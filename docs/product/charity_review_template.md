# Preregistered Charity Review Template

> This template fixes the required structure before reviews are conducted at scale. Omit no section merely because evidence is unfavorable or unavailable. Use `not assessed`, `missing`, or `insufficient evidence` rather than inventing values.

## Document control and status

- Charity ID, EIN, legal name, alternate names, and reviewed entity boundary
- Review status: scoped / draft / charity response / outside review / published / stale / withdrawn
- Cause area and programs in scope
- Research, data, and evidence cut-off dates
- Publication date, version, and next review due
- Review team, approvers, outside reviewers, and conflicts
- Charity response window and status

## One-page verdict

- **Recommendation category:** Not evaluated / Top charity / Recommended / Promising but uncertain / Strong program, limited funding gap / Not currently recommended / Insufficient evidence / Potential concern
- **Bottom line:** what an additional donation is expected to do and why
- Evidence grade and charity applicability
- Marginal cost-effectiveness range and confidence
- Room-for-more-funding range by scenario
- Financial transparency and impact transparency (separate)
- Principal reasons to donate
- Principal reasons not to donate
- Largest uncertainties and possible harms
- Last reviewed and expiry date

## Entity and program profile

Legal identity, affiliates, fiscal sponsors, service geography, target population, eligibility, delivery model, major programs, revenue model, and exactly which program receives marginal funds. Explain exclusions.

## Theory of change

Show inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → final welfare. Identify behavioral assumptions, bottlenecks, external dependencies, failure modes, and harms.

## Intervention evidence

Link the applicable evidence map. Summarize causal evidence, replication, effect range, U.S. relevance, contrary findings, grade, and differences between studied programs and this charity.

## Charity-specific implementation

Assess fidelity, staff qualifications, dosage, take-up, attrition, monitoring, outcome definitions, comparison data, subgroup results, quality controls, and independent evaluation. Distinguish reported outputs, reported outcomes, and independently supported impact.

## Financial analysis

Present at least three consistent fiscal years when available: revenue, expenses, program/admin/fundraising expenses, government grants, private donations, assets, liabilities, unrestricted assets, reserves, compensation, restrictions, and material related entities. Explain anomalies and data-quality flags. Do not use overhead as an effectiveness proxy.

## Marginal cost-effectiveness

Provide model version, funding scenario, inputs with source IDs, formulas/code, low/base/high estimates, uncertainty, sensitivity, break-even assumptions, harms, and separate human-welfare and government-fiscal results. Explain double-counting controls.

## Room for more funding

For `$100k`, `$250k`, `$1m`, and `$5m` (or justified alternatives), state spend-through, program allocation, capacity added, bottlenecks, displacement/funging, milestones, time horizon, and confidence. Identify what would change the conclusion.

## Transparency assessment

### Financial transparency
Filings, audits, reporting timeliness, entity clarity, restrictions, and explanatory quality.

### Impact transparency
Outcome definitions, denominators, follow-up, attrition, methods, limitations, evaluations, and access to negative results.

## Risks, uncertainty, and negative findings

Include model risk, evidence limitations, implementation risk, contextual unfairness, equity concerns, adverse outcomes, ideological/value sensitivity, stale data, and all material negative or null evidence.

## Recommendation decision

Apply the published category definition; state which required criteria pass, fail, or remain unknown. No recommendation may rest solely on reputation, financial ratios, scale, or a strong intervention grade.

## Charity response

Publish the response or a fair summary, requested factual corrections, project decisions, unresolved disagreements, and any analysis changes. Note non-response neutrally.

## Sources and reproducibility

Source table with IDs, URLs, retrieval dates, usage notes, extracted fields, limitations, and quality. Link raw/public documents, data extracts, model inputs, code, and downloadable report where permitted. Run the source-completeness checklist.
