# Conflict-of-Interest Policy

## Principle

Research and recommendations must be independent of financial, personal, professional, and ideological interests. Disclosure does not automatically cure a conflict; recusal, independent review, funding restrictions, or non-publication may be required.

## Covered people and interests

Staff, contractors, board members, advisors, outside reviewers, and material funders must disclose relevant interests involving a reviewed charity, affiliate, intervention vendor, or major opponent, including:

- donations or grants made, solicited, or directed;
- employment, consulting, paid speaking, or vendor relationships;
- board, advisory, volunteer, or fiduciary service;
- close family or household relationships;
- gifts, travel, hospitality, or in-kind support;
- material investments or expected financial gain; and
- public advocacy or litigation roles likely to impair impartial review.

Disclose interests from the prior three years and current negotiations. Older relationships with continuing relevance should also be reported.

## Process

1. Annual written disclosure and project-specific update before assignment.
2. A designated governance lead classifies conflicts as `none`, `disclosed`, `managed`, or `disqualifying`.
3. Management may require recusal from data collection, grading, modeling, voting, communication with the charity, or final approval.
4. Recommendation-critical work with a managed conflict receives an unconflicted second reviewer.
5. Disqualifying conflicts require reassignment; if independence cannot be achieved, no recommendation is published.
6. Material conflicts and management steps appear on the relevant public review.

## Funding safeguards

No charity may pay for or condition its own rating. Restricted funding may support broad research only when the donor cannot select conclusions, suppress negative findings, review drafts ahead of the ordinary response process, or tie payment to recommendations. Publish material funders and funding restrictions. Anonymous funding requires documented due diligence and cannot be accepted when it prevents meaningful conflict assessment.

## Gifts and donations

People with recommendation authority should not accept personal gifts from reviewed charities beyond nominal meeting refreshments. Personal donations to reviewed charities must be disclosed and generally trigger recusal from final recommendation approval during the review cycle.

## Breaches

Undisclosed conflicts are investigated, documented, and may result in correction, re-review, withdrawal of a recommendation, removal from the project, or notice to the governing body. Retaliation for good-faith disclosure is prohibited.
