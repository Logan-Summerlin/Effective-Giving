# Room for More Funding Methodology

## Purpose

Room for more funding (RFMF) asks whether a specific additional gift can expand high-impact work. It is not the size of a charity’s budget or fundraising target. Historical impact can coexist with little current RFMF, and a large stated need can coexist with weak capacity to use funds well.

## Funding scenarios

Assess, when decision-relevant, unrestricted gifts of `$100,000`, `$250,000`, `$1 million`, and `$5 million` over an explicit period. Smaller organizations may require lower scenarios. For each scenario state whether funds would:

- expand a named program or geography;
- improve quality or remove a bottleneck;
- build capacity with delayed outcomes;
- replace likely government or foundation funding;
- accumulate in reserves; or
- be redirected to another activity.

## Required evidence

Use recent budgets, audited statements, Form 990s, board-approved plans where public, job postings, utilization/capacity data, grant restrictions, pipeline/backlog evidence, reserve policy, prior growth, unit costs, and public management statements. Charity-provided information cannot be used unless a later governance process permits and documents it; until then, public sources only.

## Analysis steps

1. **Define the margin:** amount, restriction, timing, and relevant program.
2. **Estimate spend-through:** timing and probability funds become program inputs.
3. **Identify the binding constraint:** funding, staff, facilities, referrals, participant demand, regulation, leadership bandwidth, or evidence/monitoring capacity.
4. **Build an expansion budget:** incremental staff, compensation, facilities, supplies, central support, fundraising, and evaluation.
5. **Estimate marginal capacity:** participants or service units added without assuming constant average cost.
6. **Assess displacement/funging:** likely reductions in government, foundation, insurance, fee, or internal funding; alternative use of unrestricted dollars.
7. **Assess implementation risk:** pace of hiring, fidelity, quality dilution, geographic fit, and management systems.
8. **Estimate outcomes:** connect only credible incremental capacity to the cost-effectiveness model.
9. **Set a time-limited funding gap:** low/base/high amount, by when it can be used, and what evidence would update it.

## Bottleneck classification

- `funding_limited`: credible near-term expansion primarily constrained by money.
- `partially_funding_limited`: money helps, but another constraint materially limits scale.
- `nonfinancially_limited`: staff, regulation, facilities, referrals, or another constraint dominates.
- `replacement_risk`: substantial chance donations substitute for other funding.
- `reserve_accumulation_risk`: likely near-term use is unclear or reserves already exceed stated needs without explanation.
- `unknown`: public evidence is insufficient.

Classification is scenario-specific and is not a moral judgment about the organization.

## Displacement adjustment

Estimate the share of a donation that causes genuinely additional resources for the target work. Document evidence for grant crowd-out, reimbursement, restricted-fund substitution, and donor substitution. Use a range where uncertain; never default silently to 100% additionality.

## Confidence and publication

Report the source date, scenario table, spend-through range, bottlenecks, displacement range, operational milestones, major uncertainties, and confidence (`high`, `medium`, `low`, or `insufficient`). A recommendation requires evidence that at least one realistic donation scenario remains cost-effective after pessimistic RFMF assumptions.

Reassess top recommendations at least annually and sooner after major grants, contracts, leadership changes, program closures, or rapid expansion.
