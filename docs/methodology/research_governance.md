# Research and Publication Governance

## Public-data boundary

Until a formal data-governance process is approved, collect only lawfully accessible public information. Do not solicit or store confidential charity submissions, participant-level data, credentials, or unnecessary personal information. Public accessibility does not remove license, privacy, or redistribution obligations.

## Review lifecycle

1. **Selection:** publish neutral rationale; selection is not endorsement.
2. **Scoping:** preregister entity, programs, questions, outcomes, sources, and reviewer conflicts.
3. **Collection:** register sources and preserve raw material or durable references.
4. **Extraction:** distinguish source facts, analyst transforms, and judgments; apply field quality flags.
5. **Evidence mapping:** grade intervention evidence before charity conclusions.
6. **Modeling:** estimate marginal outcomes with uncertainty and RFMF scenarios.
7. **Internal review:** source-completeness, calculation, harms, fairness, and conflict checks.
8. **Outside review:** seek academic/practitioner review for methods and recommendation-critical conclusions; identify reviewers unless safety or professional constraints justify anonymity.
9. **Charity response:** provide the draft factual record and a standard response period, normally 15 business days; log requested factual changes and decisions.
10. **Publication:** publish summary, technical report, model inputs, sources, response, conflicts, and update date.
11. **Maintenance:** top recommendations at least annually; visibly mark stale work.

## Independence and reproducibility

Review templates are published before evaluation at scale. Analysts must not alter criteria merely because a favored charity performs poorly. Code and non-restricted inputs supporting public calculations are versioned and reproducible. Negative, null, and inconvenient findings are retained.

## Approval rules

No individual may alone collect decisive evidence, set model assumptions, and approve a top recommendation. Recommendation-critical factual claims require source traceability; model outputs require reproduction; `Strong` or `Potentially Harmful` evidence grades require second review. Unresolved dissent is documented.

## Charity fairness

Evaluate the correct legal entity and programs, avoid comparing unlike populations without adjustment, separate intervention evidence from implementation evidence, give organizations a factual response opportunity, and publish their response within reasonable space and safety limits. A charity’s non-response is not evidence of ineffectiveness.

## Update schedule

Each public review states evidence cut-off, data cut-off, publication date, next review due, and events that trigger early review. Expired top recommendations are suspended or clearly labeled until reassessed.
