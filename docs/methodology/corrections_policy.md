# Corrections and Research-Change Policy

## Scope

The project corrects factual, transcription, calculation, sourcing, and material presentation errors openly. It also distinguishes corrections from methodology updates, routine data refreshes, and good-faith interpretive disagreements.

## Reporting and triage

Reports should identify the page/record, disputed claim, reason, and supporting source. Acknowledge reports within five business days when operational capacity permits. Triage by potential effect on donor decisions, recommendation category, safety, legal status, and reproducibility.

## Correction classes

- **Minor:** typo or presentation defect that does not change meaning.
- **Substantive:** factual, source, extraction, or calculation error that changes analysis but not the recommendation.
- **Recommendation-affecting:** error or omitted evidence that changes category, ranking, cost-effectiveness conclusion, or funding-gap conclusion.
- **Retraction:** analysis is too unreliable to remain published.

## Required action

Correct promptly after verification. Preserve version history and add a public log entry containing discovery and correction dates, affected page/version, original error, corrected information, source, cause, recommendation effect, and prevention step. Recommendation-affecting corrections receive independent review and prominent notice on the review page.

Do not silently rewrite substantive claims. Minor copy edits may be made without individual log entries if they do not alter meaning; batch them in update history.

## Methodology changes and data refreshes

A deliberate method change is not an “error.” Publish the old and new version, rationale, effective date, affected reviews, and rerun results. Routine source updates record retrieval date and changed fields. If either changes a verdict, use the same prominence and review as a recommendation-affecting correction.

## Disagreements

A disagreement over values, model choice, or evidence interpretation is documented in reviewer notes or a charity response unless it reveals a factual or procedural error. Strong contrary arguments should be represented fairly even when the original conclusion remains.

## Withdrawals and appeals

A charity or reader may request reconsideration, but cannot veto publication. An unconflicted reviewer not solely responsible for the original decision should assess recommendation-affecting appeals. The correction log records the result and evidence considered.

## Prevention

Root-cause review may require parser tests, schema validation, source-completeness checks, second review, model snapshots, or process changes. Correction history must remain publicly accessible.
