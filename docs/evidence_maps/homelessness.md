# Homelessness Evidence Map

> **Draft Phase 3 research prototype — not a charity recommendation.** This map grades intervention categories. It does not establish a charity's implementation fidelity, marginal cost-effectiveness, or room for more funding.

## Document control

- **Map ID:** `evidence-map-homelessness`
- **Status:** `draft_internal_not_for_charity_ranking`
- **Review question:** For people experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness in the United States, which interventions improve stable housing and wellbeing relative to usual services, and what harms occur?
- **Population/geography:** Adults, families, and youth experiencing or at risk of homelessness in U.S. service and housing systems.
- **Search date:** 2026-06-12
- **Evidence cut-off:** 2026-06-12
- **Lead reviewer:** Repository implementation prototype; named human reviewer pending
- **Conflicts:** No charity-specific financial interests assessed in this phase.
- **Outside review:** Not yet externally reviewed

## Plain-language summary

This is not a charity recommendation. This map compares intervention categories, not charities. Grades describe the current direction and credibility of evidence; they are not rankings and do not establish marginal cost-effectiveness or room for more funding.

The table deliberately preserves favorable, uncertain, weak, and harmful findings. A `Strong` grade means the intervention category has comparatively credible evidence for specified outcomes—not that every provider is effective or that an additional donation is cost-effective.

## Search and selection method

- **Repositories:** PubMed, Campbell Collaboration, U.S. federal evidence repositories, National Academies, targeted citation chaining
- **Search concepts:** cause area + intervention + systematic review; cause area + randomized evaluation; intervention + harm + United States
- **Included:** Public systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized or credible quasi-experimental studies, government evaluations, and major implementation syntheses relevant to U.S. delivery.
- **Excluded:** Testimonials, charity marketing, uncontrolled output counts used as impact evidence, inaccessible claims without traceable citations, and studies with no relevant outcome.
- **Screening:** Single-reviewer Phase 3 prototype with structured extraction; grades requiring Strong or Potentially Harmful receive mandatory second review before publication or recommendation use.
- **Risk of bias:** Design, assignment, baseline balance, attrition, outcome validity, selective reporting, confounding, replication, and applicability assessed narratively under the evidence-grading method.
- **Protocol deviations:** This is a rapid single-reviewer prototype map, not a completed systematic review. Searches were targeted rather than exhaustive and no dual screening or formal meta-analysis was performed.
- **Missing-literature risks:** Publication bias, incomplete grey-literature capture, English-language bias, and fast-changing policy or drug-market contexts.

## Outcome metrics

### H-M1: Nights stably housed

- **Unit:** nights/person-year
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Define housing quality and whether institutional stays count.

### H-M2: Returns to homelessness

- **Unit:** share returning within 12–36 months
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Report denominator and administrative-data coverage.

### H-M3: Health and acute-service use

- **Unit:** ED visits, hospital days, deaths
- **Decision importance:** secondary
- **Interpretation:** Do not equate lower public spending with wellbeing.

## Intervention details

### H-I1: Long-term rental subsidy for homeless families

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Deep, sustained rental assistance reduces rent burden and enables rapid exit from shelter.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Housing stability, family separation, psychological distress, and economic outcomes over 20–37 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** HUD Family Options found large, durable reductions in homelessness and shelter use; effects on adult employment were not comparably large.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-03](#hom-03)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Adequate affordable units, landlord participation, timely placement, and benefit continuity.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Possible neighborhood constraint or benefit cliffs; monitor housing quality and involuntary moves.
- **Recommended use:** Families in shelter for whom affordability is the binding constraint.
- **Do not use as:** Do not substitute a time-limited service package when the principal barrier is unaffordable rent.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M1, H-M2
- **Open questions:** Effects in tight rental markets; Long-run child outcomes and housing quality

### H-I2: Permanent supportive housing / Housing First

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Offer permanent, non-abstinence-contingent housing with voluntary support to people with disabling conditions and chronic homelessness.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Housing stability, homelessness, health, substance use, and health-service use over 12–24+ months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Reviews consistently find substantially better housing stability; health and substance-use effects are smaller or imprecise, while some acute-care use falls.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-01](#hom-01), [HOM-02](#hom-02)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Permanent housing supply, rent subsidy, low-barrier enrollment, assertive voluntary services, and fidelity to consumer choice.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Isolation, poor housing quality, weak harm-reduction practice, and coercive service drift can erode benefits.
- **Recommended use:** People with chronic homelessness and significant disabilities who want permanent housing.
- **Do not use as:** Do not claim that housing alone reliably treats substance use or mental illness.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M1, H-M2, H-M3
- **Open questions:** Which service intensity adds wellbeing; Mortality and fentanyl-era outcomes; Youth-specific models

### H-I3: Critical Time Intervention / time-limited transition case management

**Evidence grade: Moderate (moderate confidence).** Moderate: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** A structured transition worker strengthens community ties during discharge from institutions or shelters.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Homelessness recurrence, continuity of care, and psychiatric symptoms over roughly 9–18 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Multiple trials suggest fewer homeless nights or episodes, but samples and settings are narrower than for housing interventions.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-04](#hom-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Trained case managers, phased handoff, small caseloads, and available community services and housing.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Case management cannot overcome absent housing; abrupt handoff can create discontinuity.
- **Recommended use:** High-risk transitions where concrete resources exist but coordination is weak.
- **Do not use as:** Do not use as a replacement for a needed subsidy or permanent housing.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M1, H-M2
- **Open questions:** Replication by subgroup; Optimal caseload and transition length

### H-I4: Targeted eviction prevention with flexible cash and legal triage

**Evidence grade: Promising (moderate confidence).** Promising: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Use risk screening plus short-term financial or legal help to prevent an imminent avoidable loss of housing.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Eviction filings, shelter entry, and housing stability over 6–18 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Credible local evaluations are encouraging, but targeting rules, counterfactual risk, and program bundles vary.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-04](#hom-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Fast flexible funds, valid risk triage, landlord negotiation, legal referral, and safeguards against discrimination.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Poor targeting may pay cases that would resolve anyway or exclude people with the highest barriers.
- **Recommended use:** Households with a resolvable short-term shock and credible imminent displacement risk.
- **Do not use as:** Do not treat one-time cash as sufficient for a persistent rent-income gap.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M2
- **Open questions:** Validated targeting thresholds; Comparative effects of cash, counsel, and mediation

### H-I5: Congregate transitional housing with mandatory treatment or abstinence

**Evidence grade: Mixed (moderate confidence).** Mixed: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Require residence in temporary group housing and compliance before access to permanent housing.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Program completion, housing stability, substance use, safety, and exits over 6–24 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Some participants benefit from structured settings, but comparative evidence does not support a universal treatment-first sequence and attrition can be selective.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-01](#hom-01), [HOM-04](#hom-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Safe facilities, participant choice, qualified care, clear exit pathways, and protection from discharge to homelessness.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Coercion, family separation, institutional conflict, and loss of shelter after relapse or rule violations.
- **Recommended use:** Voluntary, preference-concordant transitional options with a defined permanent-housing path.
- **Do not use as:** Do not make abstinence or treatment compliance a blanket precondition for permanent housing.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M1, H-M2
- **Open questions:** Who prefers and benefits from congregate models; Harms among non-completers

### H-I6: Punitive encampment displacement without housing offer

**Evidence grade: Potentially Harmful (moderate confidence).** Potentially Harmful: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Clear encampments through citation, arrest, property disposal, or forced movement without a viable housing placement.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Mortality, service continuity, property loss, shelter entry, and subsequent homelessness.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Direct causal estimates remain limited, but displacement disrupts medication, documents, outreach, and social ties while not resolving housing need; plausible harms dominate absent placement.
- **Key studies:** [HOM-04](#hom-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** If any site closure occurs, due process, storage, sanitation, outreach, and acceptable housing offers are minimum safeguards.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Loss of possessions and medication, trauma, criminal-legal debt, geographic displacement, and reduced service engagement.
- **Recommended use:** No recommended charitable use as a standalone homelessness intervention.
- **Do not use as:** Do not fund or describe displacement alone as housing resolution.
- **Primary metrics:** H-M2, H-M3
- **Open questions:** Prospective U.S. mortality and health studies; Effects of paired housing offers and due-process safeguards

## Research gaps and update triggers

### Priority research gaps

- Few head-to-head cost studies
- Limited mortality and quality-of-life follow-up
- Underrepresentation of rural areas, youth, LGBTQ+ people, and people exiting institutions
- Need to distinguish housing stability from treatment outcomes

### Early-update triggers

- Major systematic review or U.S. trial
- Material harm signal
- Policy or implementation change affecting applicability
- Scheduled review date

## Publication and interpretation guardrails

- Do not use to recommend a charity without a charity-specific applicability assessment.
- Do not compare grades as if they were cost-effectiveness scores.
- Separate participant wellbeing from government fiscal effects.
- Strong and Potentially Harmful grades require second review before public reliance.
