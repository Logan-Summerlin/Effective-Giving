## Plain-language summary

This is not a charity recommendation. This map compares intervention categories, not charities. Grades describe the current direction and credibility of evidence; they are not rankings and do not establish marginal cost-effectiveness or room for more funding.

The table deliberately preserves favorable, uncertain, weak, and harmful findings. A `Strong` grade means the intervention category has comparatively credible evidence for specified outcomes—not that every provider is effective or that an additional donation is cost-effective.

## Search and selection method

- **Repositories:** PubMed, Campbell Collaboration, U.S. federal evidence repositories, National Academies, targeted citation chaining
- **Search concepts:** cause area + intervention + systematic review; cause area + randomized evaluation; intervention + harm + United States
- **Included:** Public systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized or credible quasi-experimental studies, government evaluations, and major implementation syntheses relevant to U.S. delivery.
- **Excluded:** Testimonials, charity marketing, uncontrolled output counts used as impact evidence, inaccessible claims without traceable citations, and studies with no relevant outcome.
- **Screening:** Single-reviewer Phase 3 prototype with structured extraction; grades requiring Strong or Potentially Harmful receive mandatory second review before publication or recommendation use.
- **Risk of bias:** Design, assignment, baseline balance, attrition, outcome validity, selective reporting, confounding, replication, and applicability assessed narratively under the evidence-grading method.
- **Protocol deviations:** This is a rapid single-reviewer prototype map, not a completed systematic review. Searches were targeted rather than exhaustive and no dual screening or formal meta-analysis was performed.
- **Missing-literature risks:** Publication bias, incomplete grey-literature capture, English-language bias, and fast-changing policy or drug-market contexts.

## Outcome metrics

### J-M1: Victimization and serious crime

- **Unit:** incidents or victims per place/person-time
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Prefer victimization and serious harm to raw enforcement counts.

### J-M2: Legitimacy and procedural justice

- **Unit:** validated trust/fairness/cooperation scales
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Measure among people directly exposed, disaggregated by race.

### J-M3: Justice-system harms

- **Unit:** uses of force, stops, arrests, detention days, injuries
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Harms are outcomes, not implementation footnotes.

## Intervention details

### J-I1: Problem-oriented hot-spots policing with community safeguards

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Concentrate analysis and tailored problem-solving on small high-harm places rather than broad population-wide enforcement.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Crime and disorder at treated places, displacement, and community response over months to years.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** A large mostly U.S. evidence base finds small but reliable place-level crime reductions and little net displacement; problem-oriented tactics outperform simple enforcement increases.
- **Key studies:** [CJ-01](#cj-01), [CJ-04](#cj-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Accurate place analysis, tailored non-enforcement options, supervision, narrow scope, and monitoring of stops, force, and community response.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Aggressive tactics can impose concentrated burdens even when place-level crime falls.
- **Recommended use:** High-harm micro-places using the least coercive effective response and transparent equity monitoring.
- **Do not use as:** Do not infer support for zero-tolerance sweeps or indiscriminate stops.
- **Primary metrics:** J-M1, J-M2, J-M3
- **Open questions:** Long-run community effects; Tactic-level cost and harm comparisons

### J-I2: Focused deterrence / group violence intervention

**Evidence grade: Moderate (moderate confidence).** Moderate: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Deliver a clear violence-focused message to a small high-risk network alongside credible services and narrowly targeted enforcement.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Shootings, homicides, and violent offending over 1–3 years.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Systematic syntheses generally find violence reductions, but designs are often quasi-experimental and implementation packages vary.
- **Key studies:** [CJ-02](#cj-02)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Accurate and fair identification, community partnership, genuine services, procedural safeguards, and narrow focus on serious violence.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Network misclassification, collective punishment, surveillance spillovers, and inequitable enforcement.
- **Recommended use:** Concentrated serious violence where governance and service commitments are credible.
- **Do not use as:** Do not use broad gang labels or enforcement without a meaningful support offer.
- **Primary metrics:** J-M1, J-M2, J-M3
- **Open questions:** Independent replications; Effects on legitimacy and non-targeted network members

### J-I3: Voluntary restorative justice conferencing

**Evidence grade: Promising (moderate confidence).** Promising: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Facilitated voluntary dialogue seeks accountability, repair, and victim voice after responsibility is accepted.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Victim satisfaction, trauma, restitution, and repeat offending over 6–24 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Trials and reviews suggest improved victim satisfaction and possible recidivism reductions, but eligibility, offense, and facilitation matter.
- **Key studies:** [CJ-05](#cj-05)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Voluntary informed consent, trained neutral facilitators, safety screening, support persons, and no penalty for declining.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Coercion, unsafe contact, pressure to forgive, confidentiality failures, and unequal bargaining power.
- **Recommended use:** Cases where the harmed person wants the process and safety screening supports it.
- **Do not use as:** Do not mandate participation or use where power imbalance and safety cannot be managed.
- **Primary metrics:** J-M1, J-M2
- **Open questions:** Serious-violence subgroups; Long-term victim wellbeing; Facilitator fidelity

- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Scenario practice, supervisor reinforcement, policy alignment, body-camera audit, and community-defined outcomes.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.

### J-I5: Broad pedestrian stop / stop-question-frisk campaigns

**Evidence grade: Potentially Harmful (high confidence).** Potentially Harmful: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Increase police-initiated pedestrian stops broadly to detect weapons or deter crime.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Crime at places plus mental health, attitudes, later behavior, and disparities among stopped people.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** The 2023 review finds some place-level crime effects but adverse individual effects and a weaker benefit-harm profile than alternatives.
- **Key studies:** [CJ-03](#cj-03), [CJ-02](#cj-02)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** If stops occur, individualized legal basis, respectful conduct, documentation, supervision, and disparity review are essential.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Fear, trauma, reduced legitimacy, racial disparity, legal violations, and possible adverse behavioral effects.

- **Do not use as:** Do not fund quotas, indiscriminate stops, or infer citywide benefit from narrow place effects.

## Research gaps and update triggers

### Priority research gaps

- Crime outcomes dominate while legitimacy and health are undermeasured
- Few full benefit-harm and cost studies
- Need disaggregated effects and legal-compliance measures
- Implementation labels hide major tactic variation

### Early-update triggers

- Major systematic review or U.S. trial
- Material harm signal
- Policy or implementation change affecting applicability
- Scheduled review date

## Publication and interpretation guardrails

- Do not use to recommend a charity without a charity-specific applicability assessment.
- Do not compare grades as if they were cost-effectiveness scores.
- Separate participant wellbeing from government fiscal effects.
- Strong and Potentially Harmful grades require second review before public reliance.
