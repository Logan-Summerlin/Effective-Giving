# Criminal Recidivism Reentry Evidence Map

> **Draft Phase 3 research prototype — not a charity recommendation.** This map grades intervention categories. It does not establish a charity's implementation fidelity, marginal cost-effectiveness, or room for more funding.

## Document control

- **Map ID:** `evidence-map-criminal-recidivism`
- **Status:** `draft_internal_not_for_charity_ranking`
- **Review question:** For people leaving incarceration or under community supervision in the United States, which interventions reduce new offending and improve durable reintegration without causing disproportionate harm?
- **Population/geography:** Adults and youth returning from U.S. prisons or jails and people under community supervision.
- **Search date:** 2026-06-12
- **Evidence cut-off:** 2026-06-12
- **Lead reviewer:** Repository implementation prototype; named human reviewer pending
- **Conflicts:** No charity-specific financial interests assessed in this phase.
- **Outside review:** Not yet externally reviewed

## Plain-language summary

This is not a charity recommendation. This map compares intervention categories, not charities. Grades describe the current direction and credibility of evidence; they are not rankings and do not establish marginal cost-effectiveness or room for more funding.

The table deliberately preserves favorable, uncertain, weak, and harmful findings. A `Strong` grade means the intervention category has comparatively credible evidence for specified outcomes—not that every provider is effective or that an additional donation is cost-effective.

## Search and selection method

- **Repositories:** PubMed, Campbell Collaboration, U.S. federal evidence repositories, National Academies, targeted citation chaining
- **Search concepts:** cause area + intervention + systematic review; cause area + randomized evaluation; intervention + harm + United States
- **Included:** Public systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized or credible quasi-experimental studies, government evaluations, and major implementation syntheses relevant to U.S. delivery.
- **Excluded:** Testimonials, charity marketing, uncontrolled output counts used as impact evidence, inaccessible claims without traceable citations, and studies with no relevant outcome.
- **Screening:** Single-reviewer Phase 3 prototype with structured extraction; grades requiring Strong or Potentially Harmful receive mandatory second review before publication or recommendation use.
- **Risk of bias:** Design, assignment, baseline balance, attrition, outcome validity, selective reporting, confounding, replication, and applicability assessed narratively under the evidence-grading method.
- **Protocol deviations:** This is a rapid single-reviewer prototype map, not a completed systematic review. Searches were targeted rather than exhaustive and no dual screening or formal meta-analysis was performed.
- **Missing-literature risks:** Publication bias, incomplete grey-literature capture, English-language bias, and fast-changing policy or drug-market contexts.

## Outcome metrics

### R-M1: New criminal offense

- **Unit:** rearrest/reconviction share and time-to-event
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Report each definition; technical violations are separate.

### R-M2: Stable employment

- **Unit:** quarters employed and earnings
- **Decision importance:** secondary
- **Interpretation:** Employment is valuable even without recidivism effects.

### R-M3: Stable housing and treatment retention

- **Unit:** days housed; treatment retention
- **Decision importance:** secondary
- **Interpretation:** Measure participant wellbeing as well as system outcomes.

## Intervention details

### R-I1: Risk-need-responsivity aligned cognitive behavioral programming

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Structured CBT addresses criminogenic thinking and skills, targeted to assessed risk and need with trained facilitators.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Reoffending over about 12–36 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Meta-analyses generally find lower recidivism, with stronger effects when implementation quality and risk targeting are good.
- **Key studies:** [REE-01](#ree-01), [REE-02](#ree-02)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Validated assessment, trained staff, adequate dosage, quality assurance, and avoidance of over-serving low-risk participants.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Poorly delivered groups, inappropriate mixing, or coercive participation can waste time or worsen peer effects.
- **Recommended use:** People with assessed criminogenic needs, using a validated and culturally responsive model.
- **Do not use as:** Do not apply solely on offense label or use untrained generic discussion groups.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M1
- **Open questions:** Effects for women and diverse groups; Routine-practice fidelity and long-term effects

### R-I2: Correctional education with recognized credentials

**Evidence grade: Moderate (moderate confidence).** Moderate: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Provide adult basic education, secondary completion, postsecondary, or vocational education linked to usable credentials.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Recidivism, employment, earnings, and educational attainment after release.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Meta-analysis associates participation with lower recidivism and improved employment, but much evidence is quasi-experimental and programs differ.
- **Key studies:** [REE-06](#ree-06)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Qualified instructors, access across sentence lengths, transferable credits, technology, and employer-recognized credentials.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Exclusion by custody status, inaccessible materials, and credentials disconnected from labor demand.
- **Recommended use:** Learners who opt in and can continue or use credentials after release.
- **Do not use as:** Do not treat enrollment or completion alone as proof of reduced offending.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M1, R-M2
- **Open questions:** Modern randomized or discontinuity studies; Credential-specific earnings effects

### R-I3: Medication treatment for opioid use disorder during custody and reentry

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Initiate or continue methadone or buprenorphine and ensure uninterrupted community linkage after release.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Treatment retention, illicit opioid use, overdose, mortality, and reincarceration after release.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** The mortality evidence for opioid agonist treatment is strong; post-release continuity is especially important during high-risk transition periods.
- **Key studies:** [DRG-01](#drg-01), [DRG-02](#drg-02)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** All approved medications, consent, clinical staffing, prerelease enrollment, naloxone, and warm handoff without forced taper.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Forced withdrawal and treatment interruption elevate relapse and overdose risk.
- **Recommended use:** People with OUD who choose medication, including those already stable on it.
- **Do not use as:** Do not require abstinence-only detoxification or discontinue effective medication at release.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M1, R-M3
- **Open questions:** Best linkage models; Fentanyl-era dosing and retention

### R-I4: Transitional jobs as a standalone recidivism strategy

**Evidence grade: Weak (high confidence).** Weak: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Offer short-term subsidized work plus placement assistance immediately after release.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Employment, earnings, arrests, convictions, and incarceration over 1–3 years.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Large U.S. trials often improve short-term employment but show null or fading recidivism effects.
- **Key studies:** [REE-03](#ree-03), [REE-04](#ree-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Paid work, payroll reliability, employer links, coaching, and transition to unsubsidized jobs.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Low wages, abrupt job loss, and opportunity costs if participation crowds out better employment.
- **Recommended use:** Use when employment and income are valued outcomes, while testing additions that address other needs.
- **Do not use as:** Do not market a short transitional job alone as a proven recidivism intervention.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M1, R-M2
- **Open questions:** Which employer-linked models produce durable earnings; Effects of combining CBT, housing, or record relief

### R-I5: Peer navigation and comprehensive reentry support

**Evidence grade: Promising (moderate confidence).** Promising: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** A trained peer or case manager coordinates IDs, benefits, housing, care, transport, and supervision requirements.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Service connection, housing, treatment retention, employment, and recidivism over 6–24 months.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Multicomponent studies show some short-term domain gains, but causal attribution and replication are limited.
- **Key studies:** [REE-03](#ree-03)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Low caseloads, lived-experience support, flexible funds, cross-agency agreements, and participant-led goals.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Surveillance disguised as support, confidentiality breaches, and referral without actual service capacity.
- **Recommended use:** People facing simultaneous administrative and service barriers after release.
- **Do not use as:** Do not assume referrals equal receipt or that a broad bundle identifies its active ingredient.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M2, R-M3
- **Open questions:** Peer-specific randomized evidence; Effects on wellbeing and technical violations

### R-I6: Scared Straight and confrontational prison visitation

**Evidence grade: Potentially Harmful (high confidence).** Potentially Harmful: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Expose youth to prisons and confrontational messages to deter offending through fear.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Subsequent delinquency, arrest, or conviction.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Randomized evidence indicates visits can increase later offending relative to no intervention.
- **Key studies:** [REE-05](#ree-05)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** No implementation pathway makes the core fear-based model advisable; replace it with developmentally appropriate services.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Increased offending, trauma, humiliation, and normalization of criminal identity.
- **Recommended use:** No recommended use.
- **Do not use as:** Do not provide prison tours or intimidation as delinquency prevention.
- **Primary metrics:** R-M1
- **Open questions:** Monitor rebranding of similar confrontational models

## Research gaps and update triggers

### Priority research gaps

- Need consistent recidivism definitions and technical-violation reporting
- Limited evidence on participant wellbeing and family outcomes
- Sparse subgroup effects and implementation-cost data
- Need head-to-head studies of reentry bundles

### Early-update triggers

- Major systematic review or U.S. trial
- Material harm signal
- Policy or implementation change affecting applicability
- Scheduled review date

## Publication and interpretation guardrails

- Do not use to recommend a charity without a charity-specific applicability assessment.
- Do not compare grades as if they were cost-effectiveness scores.
- Separate participant wellbeing from government fiscal effects.
- Strong and Potentially Harmful grades require second review before public reliance.
