# Legal Immigrant Integration Evidence Map

> **Draft Phase 3 research prototype — not a charity recommendation.** This map grades intervention categories. It does not establish a charity's implementation fidelity, marginal cost-effectiveness, or room for more funding.

## Document control

- **Map ID:** `evidence-map-legal-immigrants`
- **Status:** `draft_internal_not_for_charity_ranking`
- **Review question:** For lawfully present immigrants and naturalization-eligible residents in the United States, which voluntary services improve language, legal access, economic security, and civic participation without exposing clients to fraud or immigration harm?
- **Population/geography:** Lawful permanent residents, refugees, asylees, and other lawfully present immigrants in the United States; legal eligibility varies by service.

- **Evidence cut-off:** 2026-06-12

- **Conflicts:** No charity-specific financial interests assessed in this phase.
- **Outside review:** Not yet externally reviewed

## Plain-language summary

This is not a charity recommendation. This map compares intervention categories, not charities. Grades describe the current direction and credibility of evidence; they are not rankings and do not establish marginal cost-effectiveness or room for more funding.

The table deliberately preserves favorable, uncertain, weak, and harmful findings. A `Strong` grade means the intervention category has comparatively credible evidence for specified outcomes—not that every provider is effective or that an additional donation is cost-effective.

## Search and selection method

- **Repositories:** PubMed, Campbell Collaboration, U.S. federal evidence repositories, National Academies, targeted citation chaining
- **Search concepts:** cause area + intervention + systematic review; cause area + randomized evaluation; intervention + harm + United States
- **Included:** Public systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized or credible quasi-experimental studies, government evaluations, and major implementation syntheses relevant to U.S. delivery.
- **Excluded:** Testimonials, charity marketing, uncontrolled output counts used as impact evidence, inaccessible claims without traceable citations, and studies with no relevant outcome.
- **Screening:** Single-reviewer Phase 3 prototype with structured extraction; grades requiring Strong or Potentially Harmful receive mandatory second review before publication or recommendation use.
- **Risk of bias:** Design, assignment, baseline balance, attrition, outcome validity, selective reporting, confounding, replication, and applicability assessed narratively under the evidence-grading method.
- **Protocol deviations:** This is a rapid single-reviewer prototype map, not a completed systematic review. Searches were targeted rather than exhaustive and no dual screening or formal meta-analysis was performed.
- **Missing-literature risks:** Publication bias, incomplete grey-literature capture, English-language bias, and fast-changing policy or drug-market contexts.

## Outcome metrics

### I-M1: Language and economic integration

- **Unit:** validated English gain, earnings, employment
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Report baseline proficiency and attendance.

### I-M2: Accurate legal completion

- **Unit:** eligible applications filed/approved without avoidable error
- **Decision importance:** primary
- **Interpretation:** Application volume alone can reward inappropriate filing.

### I-M3: Civic and social integration

- **Unit:** naturalization, voting, belonging, service access
- **Decision importance:** secondary
- **Interpretation:** Naturalization is not a proxy for all integration.

## Intervention details

### I-I1: Accessible adult English language instruction

**Evidence grade: Moderate (moderate confidence).** Moderate: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Offer sustained, level-appropriate adult English instruction with practical language use and supports for attendance.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** English skill, earnings, employment, and civic participation over multiple years.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** A U.S. lottery study found large earnings and voting gains, but it is the first standalone U.S. randomized evaluation and comes from one program.
- **Key studies:** [IMM-01](#imm-01)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Qualified teachers, level placement, sufficient hours, childcare, transport, flexible schedules, and learner retention.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Scheduling burdens, exclusion by literacy or disability, and pressure to abandon home languages.
- **Recommended use:** Adults who want English instruction, with practical supports and appropriate level placement.
- **Do not use as:** Do not promise the single-study earnings effect in every setting.
- **Primary metrics:** I-M1, I-M3
- **Open questions:** Multi-site replication; Which modalities work by baseline literacy; Wellbeing and family effects

### I-I2: Naturalization fee assistance with eligibility screening

**Evidence grade: Strong (high confidence).** Strong: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Cover application fees or help eligible applicants obtain a fee waiver, paired with accurate screening.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Naturalization applications and completions over roughly 1–3 years.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** A randomized New York study found vouchers roughly doubled application rates among eligible low-income registrants; national fee-waiver simplification evidence supports the affordability mechanism.
- **Key studies:** [IMM-02](#imm-02), [IMM-03](#imm-03)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Accredited legal screening, current fee rules, document support, and tracking through adjudication.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Ineligible or error-filled filings can waste funds or create legal risk; downstream economic gains should not be assumed.
- **Recommended use:** Eligible low-income permanent residents who want to naturalize and face a binding fee barrier.
- **Do not use as:** Do not offer filing incentives without individualized eligibility and risk screening.
- **Primary metrics:** I-M2, I-M3
- **Open questions:** Replication under current fee rules; Completion and long-term wellbeing effects

### I-I3: Qualified immigration legal representation

**Evidence grade: Promising (moderate confidence).** Promising: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Provide representation by licensed attorneys or DOJ-accredited representatives for removal defense or complex benefits matters.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Case participation, release, relief, and legal outcomes through case completion.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Large observational studies show substantial representation-outcome associations, but selection, case strength, judge, detention, and local context complicate causal interpretation.
- **Key studies:** [IMM-04](#imm-04)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Licensed or accredited providers, supervision, language access, manageable caseloads, confidentiality, and malpractice safeguards.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Poor advice, missed deadlines, conflicts, or disclosure can cause irreversible immigration harm.
- **Recommended use:** Complex, high-stakes matters where competent representation is otherwise unavailable.
- **Do not use as:** Do not use unqualified volunteers for individualized legal advice.
- **Primary metrics:** I-M2
- **Open questions:** Randomized or phased-rollout evaluations; Quality and caseload thresholds; Client wellbeing

### I-I4: Trusted navigation and benefits enrollment

**Evidence grade: Promising (moderate confidence).** Promising: based on the cited evidence, with no automatic transfer of this grade to a charity.

- **Definition and theory of change:** Use trained multilingual navigators to explain systems, schedule services, and connect eligible clients to public or community benefits.
- **Target population and setting:** U.S. populations eligible for the intervention, with subgroup and local-system applicability checked before use.
- **Comparator:** Usual services, no intervention, or the most credible alternative reported by included studies.
- **Outcomes and timeframe:** Successful enrollment, preventive care, benefit receipt, and reduced administrative burden.
- **Evidence summary/effect range:** Related community-health-worker evidence supports navigation, but immigrant-specific causal evidence and transfer across benefit rules are incomplete.
- **Key studies:** [IMM-01](#imm-01)
- **Causal strength and bias:** Grade reflects the strongest applicable causal evidence and downgrades for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, or implementation variation.
- **U.S. applicability:** Direct U.S. evidence is prioritized; transfer across populations and delivery systems remains an explicit uncertainty.
- **Cost/resource evidence:** Not standardized in Phase 3. Record direct program cost, participant time, public-system use, price year, and perspective in Phase 4.
- **Implementation and fidelity:** Current eligibility rules, privacy safeguards, warm handoffs, compensated bilingual staff, and legal referral boundaries.
- **Scale, equity, and distribution:** Track reach, take-up, exclusion, racial/ethnic disparities, disability access, rural access, and burdens on participants—not only average effects.
- **Harms:** Incorrect public-charge advice, privacy breaches, and referrals to inaccessible services can deter clients.
- **Recommended use:** Low-risk administrative navigation with clear escalation to qualified counsel.
- **Do not use as:** Do not let navigators make legal determinations outside authorization.
- **Primary metrics:** I-M2, I-M3
- **Open questions:** Immigrant-specific randomized studies; Effects of changing eligibility rules and fear

## Study inventory

### IMM-02

- **Source:** [Hainmueller et al. (2018), randomized naturalization fee-voucher study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5798346/)
- **Design/source type:** `randomized_evaluation`
- **Appraisal confidence:** `high`
- **Known limitations:** New York registrants already interested in naturalization; application is an intermediate outcome.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-06-12

### IMM-03

- **Source:** [Hainmueller et al. (2019), standardized fee-waiver application study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6708319/)
- **Design/source type:** `quasi_experimental`

- **Known limitations:** National policy discontinuity supports causal inference but relies on administrative assumptions.

### IMM-04

## Research gaps and update triggers

### Priority research gaps

- Most integration services lack causal U.S. evaluations
- Legal outcomes need quality-adjusted metrics, not filing counts
- Limited evidence by status, language, disability, and rural location
- Policy changes can rapidly invalidate applicability

### Early-update triggers

- Major systematic review or U.S. trial
- Material harm signal
- Policy or implementation change affecting applicability
- Scheduled review date

## Publication and interpretation guardrails

- Do not use to recommend a charity without a charity-specific applicability assessment.
- Do not compare grades as if they were cost-effectiveness scores.
- Separate participant wellbeing from government fiscal effects.
- Strong and Potentially Harmful grades require second review before public reliance.
