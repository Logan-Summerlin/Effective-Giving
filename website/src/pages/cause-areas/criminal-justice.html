Criminal Justice

Public defense, bail assistance, record-clearing, court navigation, and fines-and-fees relief.

Direct Service vs. Policy Advocacy: Charities in this cause area fall into two distinct categories that require different evaluation methods. Direct service organizations (public defenders, bail funds, record-expungement clinics, court navigators) serve identifiable individuals and can be evaluated on per-person outcomes and cost-effectiveness. Policy advocacy organizations work to change laws, practices, or court norms — their effects are systemic, diffuse, and far harder to quantify with conventional cost-per-outcome metrics. ClearView will apply appropriate methods to each type and will clearly label which approach is being used. We will not penalize advocacy organizations for lacking direct-service metrics, nor will we credit direct-service organizations with advocacy-level systemic impact.

Evidence Summary

The criminal justice intervention evidence base is younger and more uneven than the recidivism literature, in part because much of the relevant work happens at the intersection of legal practice and social science — a space that has historically been underfunded for rigorous evaluation. Administrative data from courts and jails is often siloed, poorly standardized, and difficult to link across systems, which limits researchers' ability to track downstream outcomes. Nevertheless, several intervention types now have enough credible evidence to support meaningful cost-effectiveness analysis, and the field has accelerated rapidly since the early 2010s.

Pretrial detention is one of the most consequential and well-studied pressure points in the system. Studies using quasi-experimental designs — including regression discontinuity approaches exploiting random judge assignment — find that even short pretrial detention spells of 2–3 days significantly increase the probability of a guilty plea, increase sentence length conditional on conviction, and cause large short-term earnings and employment losses. The mechanism is partly coercive (detained defendants accept worse plea deals to secure release) and partly practical (job loss during detention). Community bail funds address this directly; their fiscal cost is low because most bail is returned on case resolution, though the working capital required to operate a revolving fund can be substantial.

Record expungement and clean-slate reform have generated some of the most compelling recent evidence in this cause area. A landmark study of Michigan's expungement program found that petition-filers saw wage gains of approximately 25% in the years following expungement, with effects concentrated in sectors that conduct background checks. However, fewer than 10% of eligible individuals actually petition under traditional systems, meaning that automatic expungement programs have far larger population-level reach for the same per-record cost. Organizations running expungement clinics — which help individuals navigate the petition process — provide real value but serve a smaller fraction of the eligible population than automatic systems would. ClearView will note this systemic context when evaluating clinic-based charities.

Public defense quality is difficult to evaluate using conventional nonprofit metrics because the relevant comparison — what would have happened with a different attorney — is unobservable in most cases. Studies that exploit variation in public defender caseloads find that heavier caseloads are associated with worse outcomes for clients, suggesting that marginal investments in public defender capacity have positive effects. However, most nonprofit organizations working in this space are not public defender offices themselves — they fund training, technology, or capacity supplements — and isolating their marginal contribution is methodologically challenging. ClearView will use contribution-tracing analysis where possible.

The fines-and-fees landscape illustrates how a seemingly minor policy design choice can generate cascading harms. Research documents that driver's license suspensions for unpaid fines — a practice in most U.S. states — disproportionately affect low-income individuals in car-dependent communities, triggering unlicensed driving arrests, job loss, and further financial distress in a cycle that is difficult to exit. Programs that directly pay or advocate for waiver of these fees and restore licenses address a high-leverage pressure point, though rigorous impact evaluations of nonprofit-operated relief programs remain rare.

Key Metrics We Track
Cost per Jail Day Avoided

For pretrial intervention programs, we will estimate the number of pretrial detention days avoided relative to a comparison population with similar charge and risk profiles, divided by total program cost. We will use conservative comparison baselines and will not count bail amounts as program expenditure for revolving fund programs.

Cost per Conviction Avoided

For programs enhancing public defense or diversion, we will estimate the reduction in conviction probability relative to comparable defendants, converted to a per-dollar figure. Given the methodological difficulty of establishing counterfactual conviction rates, we will require strong comparison group designs before assigning this metric to any charity.

Cost per Record Cleared

For expungement and clean-slate programs, we will calculate cost per successful record clearance, distinguishing between records that meet automatic eligibility criteria and those requiring legal assistance. We will track downstream employment and earnings outcomes at 12 and 24 months where data is available.

Long-Term Employment Gain

For programs with multi-year follow-up data, we will estimate the earnings gain attributable to the intervention — record clearing, fines relief, or license restoration — relative to matched non-participants. This is the most direct measure of economic impact, though it requires administrative wage data that many organizations cannot access independently.

Research Gaps

The criminal justice intervention space has important evidence gaps that limit confident cost-effectiveness analysis and will shape how ClearView rates individual charities pending more research.

Downstream effects of pretrial detention beyond conviction. While the evidence that brief pretrial detention increases guilty plea rates is now quite robust, less is known about the full downstream effect path: how detention affects employment, housing, family stability, and future criminal justice contact over 3–5 year time horizons. Studies that follow cohorts for longer periods using linked administrative data have found persistent earnings suppression among people detained pretrial, but the cumulative cost of these harms — and therefore the potential benefit of bail intervention — is not well-quantified. Longer-term follow-up studies using wage records and housing administrative data would significantly improve cost-effectiveness estimates for bail fund programs.

Variation in expungement effects by labor market and offense type. The strong earnings gains documented in Michigan and Pennsylvania expungement studies may not generalize across all states, labor markets, and offense categories. States with weaker background-check usage by employers, or jurisdictions where sealed records remain accessible to landlords or licensing bodies, may produce smaller post-expungement gains. Similarly, the earnings effects for people with more serious or more recent records are less well-documented than for people with old, minor offenses who are the most common clinic clients. ClearView will flag this heterogeneity in evaluations of expungement programs operating in diverse contexts.

Additionality of nonprofit legal aid relative to public systems. A persistent challenge in evaluating court-focused nonprofits is determining what would have happened without them. In jurisdictions with well-funded public defender offices, a nonprofit providing supplemental attorney capacity may have minimal additionality. In jurisdictions with severely under-resourced defenders, the same organization may be the difference between representation and none. ClearView will attempt to assess local public system capacity when evaluating charities, but this contextual analysis is difficult to standardize and will require significant qualitative judgment alongside quantitative metrics.
