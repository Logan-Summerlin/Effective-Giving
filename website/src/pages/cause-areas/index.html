Cause Areas

ClearView focuses on five cause areas where U.S. donors can have large, measurable impact.

Homelessness

This cause area covers programs that prevent, reduce, and end homelessness among individuals and families in the United States, including emergency shelter, rapid rehousing, permanent supportive housing, and rental assistance. It encompasses both street homelessness and hidden homelessness such as couch-surfing and doubling-up, which disproportionately affects women, youth, and families. ClearView focuses on interventions with measurable housing-stability outcomes where the marginal cost per person housed can be estimated with reasonable confidence.

Criminal Recidivism & Reentry

This cause area covers programs that help people leaving incarceration successfully reintegrate into society and avoid rearrest, reconviction, or reincarceration. Interventions include transitional employment, housing assistance, cognitive-behavioral therapy, mentoring, and wraparound support services targeting the critical first months after release. ClearView prioritizes programs with documented reductions in recidivism rates and transparent cost-per-outcome estimates, given that each avoided reincarceration has large fiscal and human-welfare implications.

Criminal Justice

This cause area covers legal services and advocacy that reduce unjust criminal-justice outcomes, including public defense representation, pretrial bail assistance, sentence review, criminal record expungement, and court navigation for low-income defendants. Inadequate legal representation is a well-documented driver of incarceration disparities and downstream poverty, yet legal-aid capacity remains severely under-resourced relative to need. ClearView focuses on organizations where the counterfactual is clear — that is, where people would face materially worse outcomes without the service.

Legal Immigrant Integration

This cause area covers services that help lawfully present immigrants and refugees build stable, self-sufficient lives in the United States, including naturalization assistance, immigration legal aid, English-language education, occupational credential recognition, and family reunification support. Integration outcomes — employment rates, wage trajectories, civic participation, and educational attainment — are increasingly measurable through administrative data, creating opportunities for rigorous evaluation. ClearView focuses on programs addressing structural barriers rather than one-time emergency relief, where longer-term evidence of self-sufficiency gains can be tracked.

Drug Addiction & Harm Reduction

This cause area covers treatment and harm-reduction programs targeting substance use disorders — primarily opioid, stimulant, and alcohol — including medication-assisted treatment (buprenorphine/methadone), naloxone distribution, syringe service programs, peer recovery coaching, and recovery housing. Opioid overdose deaths exceed 80,000 per year in the United States, making this one of the highest-mortality, highest-cost preventable health crises in the country. ClearView focuses on interventions with documented reductions in overdose mortality and emergency department utilization, particularly where cost-per-life-saved can be estimated against alternative uses of donor funds.

Why These Cause Areas?

ClearView focuses exclusively on U.S. domestic cause areas, in contrast to most major charity evaluators that focus on global health and development. This is not because global causes are less important — the evidence is strong that the global poor can be helped at lower cost per life improved. Rather, it reflects a gap in the evaluation landscape: millions of U.S. donors want to help locally, but have little access to rigorous, evidence-based guidance on domestic charities.

The five initial cause areas were selected according to four criteria. First, evidence potential: there must be a plausible path to measuring whether the intervention works, ideally through randomized controlled trials, quasi-experimental designs, or strong observational data with robust controls. Cause areas dominated entirely by advocacy, systems change, or diffuse narrative work were excluded at this stage, not because they are unimportant, but because ClearView cannot currently evaluate them rigorously. Second, scale of suffering: each cause area must involve a large enough population of affected Americans that marginal improvements translate into meaningful aggregate impact. Third, donor neglect relative to the problem size: cause areas where philanthropic funding is already well-matched to need were deprioritized. Fourth, tractability: there must be organizations already delivering interventions where a ClearView recommendation could realistically direct additional funding toward tested programs.

These cause areas are not ranked against each other. A donor who cares most about criminal justice may find our reentry recommendations more compelling; a donor prioritizing public health may focus on harm reduction. ClearView provides comparative cost-effectiveness estimates within cause areas, not a single universal ranking across them — because such rankings require contestable moral weights that individual donors should apply for themselves.

We expect the list of cause areas to grow as research capacity expands. Areas under consideration for future inclusion include mental health services, early childhood education, workforce development, and domestic violence prevention. Any expansion will follow the same evidence-first criteria applied here.
